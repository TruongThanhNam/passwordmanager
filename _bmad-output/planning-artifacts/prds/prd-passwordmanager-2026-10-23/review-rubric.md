# PRD Quality Review — PAK Manager (iOS + Android)

## Overall verdict
This PRD is good for a personal app. It has a clear thesis ("Bảo mật trên hết, trừ khi bảo mật phá vỡ hai luồng cốt lõi") and a realistic threat model that actually drives decisions: no instant lock, no screenshot blocking, and a reboot that resets the wait. The out-of-scope list is explicit, and most FRs are testable. The risk is in the recovery story and in a few gaps in the claims. UJ-4 and TC-6 depend on the user remembering a second 15+ character export password, but the PRD never addresses this and nothing in the app prompts the user to export. Autofill, the obvious alternative to the clipboard-based core flows, is neither in scope nor a non-goal. TC-4 cannot be verified on iOS as written. Fix those three and a few done-ness ambiguities, and the PRD is ready for UX and architecture.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Most decisions are stated as decisions and come with what was given up. §2 "Nguyên tắc cân bằng" says plainly that the app does not lock on app switch and does not block screenshots, and it grounds this in the threat model. FR-11 names its accepted risk ("Đây là rủi ro đã chấp nhận"). FR-6 accepts that a reboot restarts the wait. FR-52 accepts unlimited export-password attempts. The threat model is short and honest: "Khả năng một người dùng bình thường bị tấn công có chủ đích … là rất thấp", and it says where the real protection lies ("độ mạnh của mật khẩu chính cộng với hàm dẫn xuất khóa chậm").

There are two gaps. The recovery path rests on an assumption about user behavior that is never examined. Two security mechanisms (the auto-wipe and the absolute session cap) are kept without weighing them against the threat model the PRD itself sets up. A skeptical reader would raise both points and would not find them answered.

### Findings
- **high** Recovery depends on remembering a second strong password, and the PRD never says so (§3 UJ-4, §4 F6 FR-48, §2 TC-6). UJ-4 sends a user who has *forgotten the master password* to recover via export. That export needs a separate password, "theo cùng quy tắc với mật khẩu chính (FR-2)" (15+ chars, blocklist), and possibly a different one each export ("Mỗi lần export, người dùng đặt một mật khẩu export"). A user who forgot one 15-character password months later is likely to have forgotten the other too. So the recovery path most likely fails in exactly the case it exists for. The PRD presents F6 as "sống còn" but does not raise this tension. *Fix:* State the trade-off explicitly in §2 or F6, then decide. Options: allow (or suggest) reusing the master password for export; warn at export time that this password cannot be recovered either; show the creation date on import so the user knows which password era to try. Record the choice and what it gives up.
- **medium** The cost of auto-wipe is not weighed against its marginal benefit (§4 FR-4, FR-7; §2 threat model). By the PRD's own analysis, the counter only stops attack path 1 (scripted guessing on the Login screen). Against a password of 15 or more characters, five 30-minute waits already make online guessing hopeless. Wiping at attempt 10 therefore adds little security, but it adds a real data-loss path for the stated secondary users ("kể cả người thân lớn tuổi"), children, or a mistyping owner. §2 excludes only *deliberate* wipes by a co-user, not accidental ones. *Fix:* Add one sentence that names this trade-off and why it is still accepted (for example, that it is the owner's explicit requirement from idea.txt), or reconsider the wipe.
- **medium** The absolute session cap (FR-12) is not justified by any threat path, and it drives extra cost (§4 FR-12, FR-23, FR-43). None of the three attack paths in §2 is mitigated by kicking out a user who is actively using an unlocked app. Yet FR-12 locks "kể cả khi người dùng đang thao tác", tops out at 10 minutes ("1 / 3 / 5 / 10 phút", no longer option), and creates the need for FR-23's encrypted-draft machinery. It also cuts against design principle 2 ("Một tính năng chỉ được thêm vào khi phục vụ trực tiếp các luồng ở mục 3"). *Fix:* Either state the threat FR-12 addresses (for example, a phone left unlocked on a table while the app is in the foreground) and keep it, or drop it and simplify FR-23 to cover Autolock only.

## Substance over theater — strong
Nothing here reads as furniture. There is one protagonist (Chị Lan), and she drives concrete requirements: "face" search, the copy-switch-return pattern, and Recent ordering. The Vision is specific to this product: offline, free, owner-first, family later, and "không cần thiết kế riêng cho người ít rành công nghệ". It could not be dropped into another PRD unchanged. The NFRs are product-specific, not boilerplate. NFR-7 names iCloud and Google Backup exclusion, NFR-8 names the Android 13 sensitive flag and Universal Clipboard, and NFR-9 is an explicit *negative* requirement tied to the balance principle. There is no innovation or differentiation section, and none is needed. The remaining weak spots (NFR-2 timing, NFR-4) are about done-ness, not theater, and are covered below.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is clear, and the features follow from it. §3's two core flows produce specific consequences (Autolock 3 min, Clear Clipboard 3 min, Recent newest-first), and those values reappear in FR-13, FR-34, FR-38 and FR-43. The success criteria (TC-1 to TC-6) validate the security thesis, not activity metrics, and the PRD explicitly rejects install counts ("Số lượt cài và phản hồi của người khác không phải thước đo"). TC-6 is a real counter-metric. The MVP scope is the problem-solving kind, and the scope logic matches.

### Findings
- **medium** Backup is called critical, yet nothing in the product makes it happen (§4 F6 intro, §6 store description). F6 says the export file is "bản backup duy nhất … Tính năng này là sống còn". The only mitigation, though, is a sentence in the store description ("người dùng cần export định kỳ"), and Notifications were removed. There is no export prompt after the first cards are saved, no "last export" date in Settings, and no nudge when many cards were added since the last export. TC-6 is technically met ("do lỗi của app"), but the thesis "data never lost" is not served. *Fix:* Add a small FR, such as showing "Lần export gần nhất: …" in Settings and/or a one-time prompt after N cards. Alternatively, record "no in-app backup reminders" as a non-goal with its reason.
- **low** Tablet adaptive layout is not tied to any flow (NFR-11). "trên cả điện thoại và máy tính bảng, với bố cục thích ứng" adds UX scope and App Store obligations (iPad screenshots, iPad review) without tracing to the owner's flows. *Fix:* Either confirm the owner wants tablets or limit v1 to phones (with tablet compatibility mode).

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs carry a testable consequence, and many are excellent: FR-16 (diacritics, "gõ 'd' thì khớp cả 'đ'"), FR-31 (entropy thresholds in bits), FR-37 (repeat copy does not change time, position or expiry), FR-53/54 (overwrite confirmation with card count, atomic import), and TC-3 (clock tampering included). The gaps fall on the security-critical requirements, which is where engineers most need bounds.

### Findings
- **high** TC-4 and NFR-6 cannot be verified on iOS (§2 TC-4, §5 NFR-6). "App không có quyền truy cập mạng. Kiểm tra bằng danh sách permission khi build." iOS has no network permission, so any iOS app can open sockets. As written, one of the six success gates is meaningless on one of the two platforms. Even on Android, transitive dependencies can merge `INTERNET` into the final manifest. *Fix:* Split the check by platform. Android: the *merged* release manifest contains no `INTERNET` permission. iOS: dependency audit (no networking SDKs) plus a runtime traffic capture through all flows showing zero outbound connections, consistent with the App Privacy "Không thu thập dữ liệu" declaration.
- **medium** FR-2's rejection rules are not specified tightly enough to implement or test (§4 FR-2). This matters because the PRD's own threat model rests on master-password strength. Three things are missing. (1) Which "danh sách mật khẩu phổ biến hoặc đã bị lộ", and how big? Full breach corpora run to hundreds of millions of entries, which collides with "nhúng sẵn trong app"; most common lists contain nothing of 15+ chars. (2) "Từ chối chuỗi lặp hoặc tuần tự": does this apply to the whole string or to any substring? Would "toi yeu viet nam 1234" be rejected? (3) What does the strength bar ("thanh hiển thị độ mạnh") measure? Is it the same entropy model as FR-31? *Fix:* Name the list source and a size cap (for example, top-N entries of length ≥15 from a named corpus). Define repetition and sequence as whole-string, or as a run longer than K characters. Specify the strength-bar model and its thresholds.
- **medium** FR-27 and FR-32 contradict each other on the ABC chip and lowercase letters (§4 F3). FR-27 says "chip ABC đại diện cho cả chữ hoa lẫn chữ thường. Tắt ABC thì mật khẩu chỉ còn chữ thường". Read literally, that means *only* lowercase even if 123 and !@# are on. FR-32 says "Chip ABC tương ứng công tắc Uppercase. Ở sheet, chữ thường luôn được dùng". So ABC means uppercase, and the "shared" criteria diverge: if Lowercase is off on the full screen, the sheet still uses lowercase. It is also unspecified whether using the sheet overwrites the full-screen Lowercase state. *Fix:* Restate as: "ABC = Uppercase; the sheet always includes lowercase; toggling chips in the sheet writes only Uppercase/Digits/Special to the shared state; the full-screen Lowercase toggle is not affected by the sheet."
- **medium** The unlock-time bound is vague and disagrees with the addendum (§5 NFR-2, addendum "KDF"). The PRD says "không vượt quá khoảng 1–2 giây" on "máy yếu nhất được hỗ trợ". The addendum says to calibrate to "khoảng 0,5–1 giây". The weakest supported device is undefined, because the minimum OS is deferred (NFR-11). *Fix:* Pick one number (for example, ≤1.5 s) and define the reference device or class, or bind it explicitly to the minimum OS chosen in architecture. Align the addendum.
- **medium** NFR-4's memory wipe cannot be verified as stated (§5 NFR-4). "khóa giải mã và dữ liệu đã giải mã bị xóa khỏi bộ nhớ". In garbage-collected runtimes (Swift strings, Kotlin/JVM, Dart/JS), plaintext copies cannot be guaranteed erased. *Fix:* Restate as achievable and testable: key material is held in mutable byte buffers that are zeroed on lock; all references to decrypted card objects are released; after lock, no screen shows vault data without re-login.
- **low** Lock timers during OS share sheet and file picker are unspecified (FR-51, FR-52 vs FR-13). Opening the share sheet or file picker can background the app. With Autolock at 1 min, export or import could lock mid-flow. *Fix:* State whether these system sheets pause Autolock, or that the lock waits until the operation finishes.
- **low** Some soft wording remains. FR-14 "Note chỉ hiện 2–3 dòng đầu": pick one. FR-18 "Một nút nổi bật": fine to leave placement to UX, but say so. FR-4 "số lần nhập sai còn lại": remaining before wipe, or before the next wait? *Fix:* Settle each in one phrase.
- **low** Import failure cases need user-facing outcomes (FR-54, FR-57). Atomicity is specified, but the user-facing result is not: does the user see a distinct message for wrong password vs corrupt file? FR-57 covers old→new, but what happens when an *older* app gets a newer-format file? *Fix:* Add "file from a newer version is rejected with a 'please update the app' message" and distinct error messages.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§7 "Ngoài phạm vi" is unusually explicit and does real work. It covers biometrics, merge import, instant lock, screenshot blocking, removed UI elements, and the deliberate-wipe scenario. There are 18 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, which is acceptable density for a single-owner personal app. The assumption tags are at FR-1, FR-6, FR-14 (×2), FR-16, FR-17, FR-26, FR-27, FR-29, FR-32, FR-34, FR-42, FR-44, FR-49, NFR-2, NFR-8, NFR-12 and §6 privacy policy, and they will be triaged later. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` items (store name availability, Apple export compliance) sit at real release checkpoints. The problems are a few large omissions that a reader would otherwise have to infer.

### Findings
- **high** OS autofill is neither in scope nor a non-goal (§3, §7). Both core flows (UJ-1, UJ-2) and the whole balance principle (no lock on switch, clipboard exposure, FR-34 clearing) exist because credentials move via copy-paste. iOS (AutoFill credential provider) and Android (Autofill service) both offer offline autofill that would remove most clipboard exposure. This is the first objection any reviewer of a password manager will raise, and §7 is silent on it. *Fix:* Add an explicit non-goal ("Tự động điền (AutoFill/Autofill service) — ngoài phạm vi v1 vì …") with the reason (simplicity, effort, extension complexity). Alternatively, raise it as a real open question.
- **medium** How the vault first gets filled is not addressed (F6, §7). Import accepts only the app's own export format, with overwrite only. There is no import from Chrome, Google Password Manager, iCloud Keychain or CSV, and §7 does not list it as a non-goal. For the stated motivation ("mỗi người phải đăng ký rất nhiều ứng dụng"), onboarding means typing every account by hand, while FR-12 re-locks at least every 10 minutes and demands a 15+ character password each time. *Fix:* List "Import từ trình quản lý mật khẩu khác / CSV" as an explicit non-goal, or add it. Also acknowledge the effect of the session cap on initial data entry.
- **medium** Store release prerequisites are missing (§6). There is no mention of developer-account costs (Apple Developer Program is a recurring annual fee; Google Play has a one-time fee) for a "miễn phí hoàn toàn, phi thương mại" project. Google Play's requirement that new *personal* developer accounts run a closed test with a minimum number of opted-in testers for 14 days before production access is also missing (currently 12 testers; verify). That requirement fits the "share with family" plan, but it is a gating step. *Fix:* Add a short "Điều kiện phát hành" list: accounts and fees, the Play closed-testing requirement, privacy policy URL, and (if tablets are kept) iPad screenshots.
- **low** §8 "Câu hỏi mở và giả định" holds no actual questions (§8). Real open items are scattered elsewhere: minimum OS (NFR-11), Apple export-compliance type (§6), store name availability (§6). *Fix:* Collect them in §8 as a short list, alongside the Assumptions Index (see Mechanical notes).

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is the start of a chain: the addendum explicitly hands work to bmad-ux and architecture. So clean extraction matters. FR IDs are unique, UJs have a named protagonist, and features map cleanly to F-sections. The weak point is vocabulary. There is no glossary, and the mixed English/Vietnamese naming makes some terms ambiguous when a section is lifted on its own.

### Findings
- **medium** There is no glossary, and key terms drift (whole PRD). "vault" is used in FR-11, FR-41, NFR-2, NFR-3, NFR-4a and NFR-10 but never defined. "Tạo mật khẩu" names both a tab/screen (FR-24, FR-42) and the button inside the edit form that opens the *sheet* (FR-21). "Màn hình đầy đủ" (FR-27, FR-31, FR-32) is an unannounced synonym for "Màn hình Tạo mật khẩu". "Recent" / "Recent Passwords", "Autolock" / "Autolock Vault", "phiên" / "Giới hạn phiên", and "mật khẩu chính" / "Main Password" alternate. *Fix:* Add a short glossary (vault, card, bản nháp card, màn hình Tạo mật khẩu, sheet tạo mật khẩu, Recent Passwords, phiên, Autolock, mật khẩu chính, mật khẩu export) and rename the FR-21 button (for example, "Tạo ngẫu nhiên") so it does not collide with the tab name.
- **low** The screenshot references that FR-24 and FR-25 depend on have no paths (FR-24, FR-25, FR-35, FR-43). The PRD cites `password_generator.jpg`, `edit_pass.jpg`, `recent_password.jpg` and `setting.jpg`, and FR-24/25 depend on them ("giữ nguyên toàn bộ thành phần trong ảnh tham chiếu"). The files actually live at `screens/` in the repo root. *Fix:* Use repo-relative paths (`screens/password_generator.jpg` etc.).

## Shape fit — strong
This is a solo/hobby product shipped to public stores, with meaningful UX. Four short UJs with a named protagonist, plus a capability-style FR list, is the right shape. The UJs are load-bearing (they set the 3-minute defaults and Recent ordering), not decorative. Rigor is light where it should be: no personas section, no market analysis, success defined by the owner's satisfaction plus verifiable security gates. Pushing UX-open items and implementation details (KDF parameters, monotonic clocks, Keychain behavior) into the addendum keeps the PRD clean. The FR count (~58) is high for a personal app, but each FR carries a real decision, so it is not over-formalized. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- **FR numbering is not sequential.** FR-34 sits in §F-X after FR-41, and FR-41a and NFR-4a exist. This is already planned for renumbering in polish. Re-check all FR-34 references (FR-43) after renumbering. The "F-X" section label should also get a proper F-number.
- **Broken cross-reference:** FR-11 says export passwords are covered "xem F5", but export is F6 (FR-48). F5 is Settings.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** No index exists. §8 only says assumptions "sẽ được rà soát ở bước Finalize". There are 18 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (listed under Scope honesty) and 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, and none are indexed. Add the index during triage.
- **PRD/addendum inconsistency:** Unlock time is "1–2 giây" (NFR-2) vs "0,5–1 giây" (addendum KDF).
- **Name drift:** The addendum's frontmatter title is "Password Manager — Addendum", while the product is "PAK Manager".
- **UJ protagonist:** All four UJs carry Chị Lan inline. OK.
- **Required sections:** All present for the stakes (vision, success criteria with counter-metric, flows, FRs, NFRs, release, out-of-scope). Only the glossary and the assumptions index are missing.
